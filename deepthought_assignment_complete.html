Task 1

Campaign Viability Analysis — GlowNest

GlowNest sells a Premium Face Serum at ₹800. Monthly ad spend is ₹3L generating 1,500 ad-attributed sales, alongside 2,000 organic sales. Fixed costs are ₹1.5L/month.

Q1 — Unit Economics (Ad-Attributed Sales)

MetricFormulaCalculationResult
Gross Margin / UnitSP − COGS800 − 280₹520
Amazon Fee / UnitSP × 12%800 × 0.12₹96
Ad Cost / UnitAd Spend ÷ Ad Units3,00,000 ÷ 1,500₹200
Net Margin / Ad UnitGM − Fee − Ad Cost520 − 96 − 200₹224
Interpretation

After all variable costs, GlowNest earns ₹224 per ad-driven sale — a 28% net margin. Healthy for premium skincare, but the ad cost per unit (₹200 = 25% of selling price) is something to watch as campaigns scale.

Q2 — Monthly Profitability

Key Insight

Organic sales (₹8.48L) contribute more than 2× the ad channel (₹3.36L). This is a healthy sign — GlowNest is not purely dependent on ad spend to generate revenue. The organic base provides profitability cushion.

Q3 — ACOS and TACOS

ACOS = Ad Spend ÷ Ad Revenue × 100 = 3,00,000 ÷ (800 × 1,500) × 100 = 3,00,000 ÷ 12,00,000 × 100 = 25% TACOS = Ad Spend ÷ Total Revenue × 100 = 3,00,000 ÷ (800 × 3,500) × 100 = 3,00,000 ÷ 28,00,000 × 100 = 10.71%
ACOS 25% — Borderline Acceptable ✓

For a brand with 65% gross margin (₹520 on ₹800), the unit-level break-even ACOS is 53% (= GM% − Amazon Fee%). At 25%, GlowNest has significant headroom. However, the target should be 15–20% as campaigns mature and efficiency improves.

TACOS 10.71% — Healthy ✅

Industry benchmark for TACOS is typically 8–15% for a growing brand. Under 11% is strong. The large organic base (2,000 units) pulls TACOS down, signalling brand equity building. This is the metric to track month-over-month.

Q4 — Break-Even Ad Spend

Setting Total Monthly Profit = 0, holding ad units constant at 1,500: [(520 − 96 − X/1500) × 1500] + [424 × 2000] − 1,50,000 = 0 [6,36,000 − X] + 8,48,000 − 1,50,000 = 0 13,34,000 = X Break-Even Ad Spend = ₹13,34,000/month Unit-level break-even (where ad sales alone cover costs): Net Margin = 0 → Ad Cost/Unit = ₹424 Break-even Spend = 424 × 1,500 = ₹6,36,000 → ACOS = 53%
Practical Guardrail

While the theoretical ceiling is ₹13.34L (subsidised by organic), the operational rule should be: never let ACOS exceed 53% (unit-level break-even). At the current ₹3L spend, GlowNest is well within safe territory. The meaningful risk threshold is ACOS > 40%, which starts significantly compressing unit margins.

SCENARIO

MetricBeforeAfterChange

What I Would Recommend to GlowNest

1. Pause broad-match campaigns and concentrate budget on exact/phrase match for top 10 converting keywords — this is the most common cause of ACOS degradation.

2. Negative keyword audit immediately — worsening ACOS signals irrelevant click spend.

3. Check listing CTR and CVR — if conversion rate dropped (often linked to listing quality or competitor pricing), ad efficiency falls even with good bids.

4. Do not panic-cut ad spend entirely — organic rankings are partially sustained by ad activity. A complete pause can hurt keyword ranking and compound the problem.

5. Set a 30-day ACOS recovery target of ≤30% as a measurable milestone to report to the client.

SCENARIO

Q6 — Double Ad Spend to ₹6L, Ad Units to 2,500

Ad Cost per Unit = 6,00,000 ÷ 2,500 = ₹240 Net Margin per Ad Unit = 520 − 96 − 240 = ₹184 New Profit = (184 × 2,500) + (424 × 2,000) − 1,50,000 = 4,60,000 + 8,48,000 − 1,50,000 = ₹11,58,000 New ACOS = 6,00,000 ÷ (800 × 2,500) × 100 = 30% New TACOS = 6,00,000 ÷ (800 × 4,500) × 100 = 16.67%

Verdict: Marginally Yes, But Proceed Carefully

Profit increases but with worsening efficiency — TACOS crosses 15% and enters the "watch closely" zone. The marginal ROI of 41% is positive but not compelling.

My advice: Don't jump from ₹3L to ₹6L in one step. Increase to ₹4L first, monitor ACOS and conversion data for 3–4 weeks, then decide. The Amazon ad algorithm needs time to allocate larger budgets efficiently — front-loading can cause ACOS to spike before stabilising, and the real results only become visible in weeks 6–8.

V3 Pattern Observations
Pattern 1 — Report Design Problem (Not a Client Problem)

Brand A and Brand C asked the exact same question about report columns in the same week — independently. Two out of three clients can't read our standard weekly report. This is not a client education issue; it's a report design issue. I'd recommend we spend 2 hours revising the report template: rename columns to plain English, add a one-line legend at the top, and include a "plain language" summary above the data. This is a one-time fix that prevents the same friction for every future client.

Pattern 2 — Invisible Work = Churn Risk

UrbanPulse consumed 12 hours this week, but from the client's view, "nothing is happening." Across all three brands, our effort is invisible unless a KPI moves. I'd suggest a lightweight "3 things we did this week + why" section in every weekly report — not to justify ourselves, but because clients who understand what we're doing are less likely to churn when numbers temporarily dip.

Pattern 3 — Untapped Channel (NestWell / Flipkart)

NestWell has an active Flipkart storefront with zero ad spend. If their Amazon ROAS is 3.5, Flipkart's lower CPCs (less mature ad platform) could produce even better efficiency. This is either a cross-sell conversation or at minimum a question: "Was Flipkart advertising a deliberate choice to deprioritise?" If not, this is a revenue opportunity they've been leaving on the table.

Task 3 — Part B

MD Talking Points — UrbanPulse (Men's Grooming)

Context: UrbanPulse MD requests a 15-minute call on Amazon growth plans for next quarter. I chose UrbanPulse because the gap between current performance (ROAS 2.1) and growth potential makes this the most strategically interesting conversation — and the highest-stakes account to retain.

1. Where We Stand — Honest Framing (2 min)

"Your Amazon channel is in Month 2 of a structural rebuild. The previous agency's Month 1 ROAS numbers looked stronger, but the foundation was broad targeting generating low-quality traffic. We've rebuilt for precision. The ROAS will reflect this in weeks 6–10. I want to be transparent about that timeline so we're aligned."

2. What the Category Data Shows (3 min)

"Men's grooming on Amazon India grew roughly 38% last year. Your category is competitive — the top 3 brands are investing in Sponsored Brand Video ads and running promotions on high-traffic events (Prime Day, Diwali). UrbanPulse isn't doing either yet. That's not a criticism, it's a window: the opportunity to gain share before those placements become more expensive is open right now, not next year."

3. The Listing Problem = Revenue Waiting to Be Unlocked (3 min)

"There's one lever that no amount of ad spend can fix on its own: if the product page doesn't convert, we're paying to bring people to a door they won't walk through. Your top SKUs have weaker images than direct competitors in the same search results. A one-time investment in lifestyle photography — typically ₹30–50K — can improve conversion rate by 15–25%. At your current traffic, that's ₹2–3L in additional monthly revenue with zero incremental ad spend. It's the highest-ROI action available to you right now."

4. The 90-Day Growth Picture (4 min)

"If we reach ROAS 3.0+ by Month 3 (which is the expected outcome of the restructuring we've done), the business case for scaling ad spend to ₹5–6L/month becomes clear. That trajectory could take your Amazon revenue to approximately ₹X by Month 6. I'd like to model this properly for you — can you share your total Amazon revenue for the last 3 months so I can build a realistic projection rather than an estimate?"

5. Questions I'd Ask the MD

• "Where does Amazon fit in your overall strategy — is it your primary D2C growth channel, or one of several equal channels?"

• "Is there a budget available for creative (listing images, A+ content) separate from ad spend? That's the single highest-leverage fix right now."

• "Are there new product launches planned in the next 6 months? Launch campaigns benefit enormously from early ad investment to accelerate keyword ranking — it's significantly cheaper to buy rank at launch than to recapture it later."

• "Is Flipkart an active channel for you? We've seen brands in adjacent categories find Flipkart's ad platform more efficient right now due to lower competition."

How My Thinking Shifted (Status Update → MD Talking Points)

In the status update for the team lead, I was thinking operationally: ROAS numbers, hours invested, client sentiment, what to do next week. When I shifted to preparing for the MD call, I stopped asking "how are the campaigns doing?" and started asking "where is this business going, and what is blocking it?"

The listing image observation is a small operational flag in a status update. In an MD conversation, it becomes: "there's ₹2–3L/month in revenue sitting here that doesn't need a single rupee of ad spend." Same data point, completely different frame.

Information I'd need that I don't currently have: UrbanPulse's total Amazon revenue (not just ad-attributed), their gross margin structure, whether they have D2C or Flipkart presence, their target CAC, and any upcoming product launches. Without these, the growth story is incomplete — I can show the direction but not a precise destination.
